— La cabecera —

Visas & residency

Six routes worth knowing. The right pick depends on whether you have a Spanish employer, freelance clients abroad, or capital. Below: the routes, the costs, and who should take which.

The shortcut: If you have a Spanish job offer above €40k → HQP. If you run a UK/global business from a laptop → DNV. Both unlock the Beckham Law 24% flat tax.

The six routes — 2026

Per-person matrix

  • Calum CD — HQP via a Spanish employer (UNWTO, AECID-adjacent, consultancy). Fallback: DNV if remote-only contract.
  • Rose RH — HQP via IQVIA/MSD/AstraZeneca Madrid hub, or family-reunification on Calum's HQP.
  • Jack JW — HQP managerial (€54,142+) or EU Blue Card. PE roles comfortably clear both. Beckham almost certainly worth electing.
  • Momina MW — DNV if business income proves >€2,849/m, else Autónomo. DNV is faster and keeps tax simpler.

Beckham Law — the tax prize

  • 24% flat on Spanish-source employment income up to €600,000 (47% above)
  • Foreign income (outside Spain) not taxed in Spain during the regime
  • 6 years total (the move year + 5 following)
  • Apply within 6 months of social security registration
  • 2026 Startup Law update: includes DNV holders and dependants under 25

Documents — common pack

  • Passport (6m validity) + 2 photos
  • ACRO criminal record check (UK) — apostilled + sworn-translated
  • Medical certificate (UK GP) — apostilled + translated
  • Proof of health insurance (DNV/NLV: no co-pays, no waiting periods)
  • Proof of income / contract / business activity
  • Apostilled marriage and birth certificates (for spouse + child applications)

Where to live

For young families in their 30s and 40s with one or two small children: walkable, plaza-anchored neighbourhoods where the playground, the panadería and the wine bar are all within five minutes. These are the five that consistently come up.

Avoid (for this profile)

  • Gran Vía / Sol — tourist throughfare, noisy, no plazas worth their name.
  • Malasaña core — fun, but late-night noise & few playgrounds. Better as a destination than an address.
  • Far suburbs (Las Rozas, Tres Cantos) — fine if a specific school dictates it; otherwise you lose the very thing you came for.
Rental reality check — Rents up ~11% YoY into 2026. Landlords typically ask for 2–3 months deposit plus a Spanish guarantor (avalista). Workarounds: Avalbox / Avaloop rental guarantees · 6 months rent up-front · or start with a furnished 6-month rental via Spotahome while you find your feet.

Job market

Four people, four different sectors. Each is well-served in Madrid; the routes in are different.

Private equity Jack

Madrid is Iberia's PE capital. Most international funds run their Iberian deal teams from here.

  • Internationals: CVC, KKR, Cinven, EQT, Apollo, Bain Capital, Bridgepoint, Carlyle
  • Spanish funds: Alantra, Magnum, Portobello, ProA, MCH, GED, Nazca, Suma Capital
  • Adjacent: McKinsey PEPI, BCG, Bain consulting; Big Four transaction services
  • Comp: VP €120–€180k base + 50–100% bonus + carry; Principal €180–€280k + carry

Health data & pharma Rose

  • Industry hubs: IQVIA Iberia, MSD, AstraZeneca, GSK Spain, Pfizer, Roche
  • Spanish health-tech: Savana Médica, Quibim, Mediktor, Methinks
  • Public/academic: ISCIII (Carlos III Health Institute), IS Global, CNIC
  • Comp: Senior epidemiologist / data scientist €55–€90k; principal €90–€140k

Evaluation & public policy Calum

  • Multilaterals: UNWTO (HQ in Madrid), AECID, OEI, FIIAPP
  • Consultancies: Red2Red, Inforegio, Technopolis Iberia
  • Adjacent: ISCIII (for health-evaluation), Elcano, Esade Geo
  • Spanish B2+ matters here; English-only roles exist at UNWTO and international NGOs

Self-employed / founder Momina

  • Status: Autónomo (RETA) or DNV — DNV preferred if income >€2,849/m and clients abroad
  • Communities: Impact Hub Madrid, Utopicus, La Industrial, Madrid Female Founders
  • Year-1 perk: Autónomo "tarifa plana" — €80/m social security for 12 months
  • Tax: Quarterly IVA (303) + IRPF (130); annual return June

Healthcare — DNV/NLV-compliant insurance

DNV and NLV both require private insurance with no co-pays and no waiting periods. Buy before the visa interview.

  • DKV — most common DNV pick · €60–€90/m/adult
  • Sanitas — broad network incl. Hospital Sanitas La Moraleja (English consultants) · €80–€100/m
  • Adeslas — Spain's largest · €55–€85/m
  • Mapfre Salud — strong family plans · €65–€90/m

Once on Spanish social security, you can drop private — but most expat parents keep Sanitas for paediatrics.

Schools — the British curriculum cluster

Apply 1–2 years ahead for the top schools. Most cluster around Pozuelo, La Moraleja, and Soto de Viñuelas.

For Jack & Momina's son (almost 1): focus first on escuelas infantiles (0–3) — public €150–€300/m, private/bilingual €500–€1,200/m. British Council Early Years takes from 18 months.

Banking — open one before you need it

  • BBVA · easiest with just NIE, strong English app
  • Santander · familiar to UK arrivals, branches everywhere
  • Sabadell · ex-TSB owner, English-speaking onboarding
  • Openbank · Santander's digital arm, fully online, English
  • N26 / Revolut / Wise · keep as bridge accounts in the first weeks

Tax — Beckham vs general

  • Beckham (HQP/DNV): 24% flat on Spanish-source up to €600k · 47% above · 6 years
  • General: progressive 19–47%, Madrid region is one of the lowest in Spain
  • Autónomo: €80/m year-1 social security, then €300–€500 based on income; quarterly IVA (303) + IRPF (130)
  • UK side: file P85 on leaving · CA3837 if remaining self-employed via DNV · double-tax treaty prevents being taxed twice

Spanish — how much you need

  • International firms (PE, IQVIA, McKinsey): English-only is fine
  • Spanish public / clinical / Spanish PE: B2+ Spanish
  • Daily life: A2 minimum · B1 makes everything dramatically easier
  • Bureaucracy: police stations rarely speak English — bring a Spanish speaker for TIE biometrics

First 3 months — checklist

  • NIE — from UK consulate or in Spain
  • Spanish bank account — N26/Revolut first, then BBVA/Santander
  • Furnished rental for 6 months while finding feet
  • Empadronamiento at the Junta Municipal (unlocks everything else)
  • TIE at police station, within 30 days of arrival
  • Tarjeta Sanitaria at Centro de Salud
  • Dog registration at Junta Municipal + Spanish EU Pet Passport
  • Nursery/school application
  • Beckham Law election within 6 months of social security registration
  • HMRC: P85 leaving UK + CA3837 if self-employed via DNV

Pets & family

Two dogs from the UK. One nearly-one-year-old. The logistics that aren't optional.

Moving the dogs — post-Brexit

UK pet passports are no longer accepted. Start 8–12 weeks before travel.

  1. Microchip — ISO 11784/11785 compliant 15-digit. Must be implanted before rabies vaccination.
  2. Rabies vaccination — at least 21 days before travel.
  3. Animal Health Certificate (AHC) — from a UK Official Veterinarian, within 10 days of travel.
  4. Travel — best route for medium/large dogs: Eurotunnel + drive or Portsmouth–Bilbao ferry (pet-friendly cabin, book early).
  5. On arrival — register at the local Junta Municipal (~€20), get a Spanish EU Pet Passport from a Madrid vet.

Most family breeds are not on the PPP (potentially dangerous) list. Staffies, Rotties, Akitas are — they need extra liability insurance.

The baby — nurseries (0–3)

  • Public escuelas infantiles — €150–€300/m, income-banded · long waiting lists · empadronamiento required
  • Private bilingual — €500–€1,200/m · widely available · much shorter waits
  • British Council Early Years (Pozuelo) — accepts from 18 months · long wait · €700–€1,000/m
  • Hastings School Nursery — from 18 months · British curriculum
Register the baby at the empadronamiento on arrival — it's the gateway to public nursery, healthcare, and eventually school points.

Family-friendly Madrid life

  • Restaurants are universally child-friendly. High chairs everywhere. Late dinners with a baby in a buggy are normal.
  • The Madrid metro is buggy-friendly (lifts on most major stations); buses kneel.
  • Under-7s travel free on public transport.
  • Pharmacies are well-stocked for infant essentials; Doctoralia finds English-speaking paediatricians.
  • Doggy daycare ~€15–€25/day; many cafés and shops are dog-friendly.

## 2. Housing

### Rental market overview

Madrid rents rose ~11% in 2025, with average asking ~€21.30/m². Demand is high; agencies are professional but landlords often want a Spanish guarantor or 2–3 months' deposit upfront.

**Furnished vs unfurnished:**
- **Furnished (amueblado):** Easier for new arrivals; usually 1-year lease with break clauses after 6 months
- **Unfurnished (sin amueblar):** Cheaper long-term; standard lease is 5 years for individual landlords or 7 years for companies, with annual price-index renewal

**Typical documents required:**
- Passport + visa or TIE
- Empadronamiento certificate (may be required after signing)
- 3 most recent payslips or accountant's letter (autónomos)
- Last 3 months' Spanish bank statements
- Spanish guarantor (avalista) earning 3× the rent — **hardest to get as a new arrival**
- Deposit (fianza): 1 month
- Additional guarantee deposit (1–2 months): often required from foreigners; protected under Spanish law

**Workarounds for new arrivals without a guarantor:**
- Rent through expat-focused agencies (Spotahome, Idealista's furnished listings, Engel & Völkers Madrid)
- Many landlords accept 6 months' rent in advance instead of a guarantor
- Some employers offer relocation packages that cover the guarantee
- Companies like **Avalbox** and **Avaloop** provide third-party rental guarantees

## 4. Healthcare

### The Spanish healthcare system

Spain has one of Europe's most highly regarded systems — universal, mostly free at the point of use, with strong primary care and excellent secondary/tertiary hospitals. Madrid is one of the better-served regions.

**System structure:**
- **Public — Sistema Nacional de Salud (SNS), Madrid branch SERMAS**: Free at point of use once registered
- **Private — supplementary or substitute insurance**: Used by ~25% of Madrileños for faster appointments and choice of consultant

### Getting registered: Tarjeta Sanitaria

1. Obtain NIE/TIE
2. Register at the town hall (empadronamiento)
3. Register with social security (Seguridad Social) — your employer does this if you're on HQP; you do it yourself on the DNV/Autónomo route via RETA
4. Apply for **Tarjeta Sanitaria Individual (TSI)** at your local SERMAS health centre (Centro de Salud)
5. You're assigned a primary care doctor (médico de cabecera)

**Timeline:** ~2–4 weeks from arrival to having the TSI in hand.

### For visa-required private insurance (DNV, NLV, Autónomo)

The DNV and NLV both require **comprehensive private health insurance with no co-payments and no waiting periods**. Standard expat policies often won't meet this — use a DNV/NLV-compliant policy.

**DNV-compliant insurers:**
- **DKV** — most commonly used for DNV applications; ~€60–€90/month/adult
- **Sanitas** — broad hospital network including British Hospital of Madrid (Hospital Sanitas La Moraleja); ~€80–€100/month/adult
- **Adeslas** — largest Spanish private insurer; ~€55–€85/month/adult
- **Mapfre Salud** — solid family plans; ~€65–€90/month/adult
- **Asisa** — bigger in Andalusia, has Madrid network too

For families with young children, **Sanitas** is the most commonly recommended; its English-speaking GP service in Salamanca is popular with expats.

### Practical tips
- Get private insurance set up **before** the visa interview — proof of policy is part of the application pack
- Once on Spanish social security, you can drop private insurance, but many expats keep it for paediatrics and dentistry
- **NHS S1 form** is only relevant if you draw a UK state pension — not applicable to anyone in this group

### British Hospital of Madrid (Sanitas La Moraleja)
- Av. Manuel de Falla, 1, Alcobendas
- English-speaking consultants, popular with British and American expats
- Covered by Sanitas and most international policies

## 5. Schools (Mamina & Jack's son)

The son is almost one — so the immediate question is **infant care / nursery (escuela infantil)**, not schools. But it's worth understanding the school pipeline because British school waiting lists are long.

### Nurseries (Escuela Infantil) — ages 0–3
- **Public nurseries** (Escuelas Infantiles Públicas, run by Comunidad de Madrid): subsidised; €150–€300/month based on parental income; long waiting lists; admission criteria favour residents (so empadronamiento matters)
- **Private nurseries**: €500–€1,200/month; English-language and bilingual options widely available
- **British Council School Early Years** — accepts children from 18 months; bilingual; €700–€1,000/month; long waiting list
- **Hastings School (Hortaleza, La Moraleja, Madrid Centro)** — Nursery from 18 months; British curriculum
- **St George's British International School Madrid** — Nursery from age 2; British curriculum

### British curriculum primary schools (5+)
| School | Location | Age range | Approx. fees/year |
|---|---|---|---|
| **British Council School** | Pozuelo de Alarcón | 3–18 | €11,000–€15,000 |
| **Hastings School** | 3 campuses | 18 months – 18 | €9,000–€14,000 |
| **St George's British International** | Tres Cantos | 2–18 | €9,000–€13,000 |
| **The English Montessori School** | La Moraleja | 1–18 | €10,000–€13,000 |
| **Runnymede College** | La Moraleja | 3–18 | €13,000–€18,000 |
| **King's College Madrid** | Soto de Viñuelas | 3–18 | €12,000–€16,000 |
| **International College Spain (ICS)** | Madrid (IB) | 3–18 | €13,000–€20,000 |

### Application process
- **Apply 1–2 years in advance** for top schools (Runnymede, British Council School)
- Required documents: child's birth certificate (apostilled, sworn-translated), vaccination records, school reports if any, passport
- A visit and parent interview are common; deposit on acceptance (often non-refundable)

### Public school route
- Free, high-quality, but mainly Spanish-language
- Bilingual public schools (Programa Bilingüe) teach ~30% of curriculum in English
- Admission via Comunidad de Madrid points system; empadronamiento and proximity to the school are the main criteria

## 6. Moving the dogs

Both households are bringing one dog from the UK. Post-Brexit rules apply — UK pet passports are **no longer accepted** for entry to Spain.

### Required documents
- **ISO 11784/11785 compliant 15-digit microchip** (must be implanted **before** rabies vaccination)
- **Rabies vaccination**, given at least **21 days before travel** (and not expired)
- **Animal Health Certificate (AHC)** issued by an official OV in the UK **within 10 days** of travel — replaces the old pet passport
- Tapeworm treatment is **not required** for entry to Spain (only required for return to UK)

### Timeline
Start **8–12 weeks before** travel:
1. Confirm microchip is ISO-compliant; replace if older
2. Rabies vaccination at OV (or check existing booster validity)
3. Book OV appointment for AHC, 1–10 days before travel
4. Choose route (see below)
5. After arrival in Spain, **register the dog** at the local Junta Municipal de Distrito and obtain a Spanish EU Pet Passport from a local vet (€30–€60)

### Travel routes

| Route | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| Ferry + drive (Portsmouth–Bilbao / Plymouth–Santander) | Dog stays with you the whole way; many British expats prefer this | 24h crossing; need a pet-friendly cabin (book early); drive from Bilbao/Santander to Madrid is 4–5h |
| Eurotunnel + drive | Dog in car the whole way; very smooth | Long drive (~24h with overnight stop); expensive fuel/tolls |
| Fly cargo (most UK→Madrid flights only carry cargo, not cabin, for medium/large dogs) | Faster | More stressful for the dog; airline crate spec strict; cargo costs €400–€1,200; specialist pet shipper recommended (e.g. PetAir UK, Airpets) |
| Fly in cabin (small dogs <8kg only) | Best for small dogs | British Airways and Iberia do not allow pets in cabin from UK; you'd need a continental connection (Lufthansa via Frankfurt allows in-cabin) |

**For a medium/large dog, ferry+drive or Eurotunnel+drive is usually best.**

### Madrid life with a dog
- Dogs must be **leashed** in all public spaces unless in a designated off-leash zone
- Compulsory **microchip registration** at the Madrid municipal database within 30 days of arrival (~€20)
- Compulsory **third-party liability insurance** for dogs on the PPP (potentially dangerous breeds) list — Staffies, Rotties, Akitas etc. Most family dogs are not on this list
- Doggy daycare is widely available (~€15–€25/day; €250–€350/month full-time)
- Many cafés, restaurants and shops are dog-friendly; metro allows dogs on a leash in off-peak hours

## 7. Banking

### Opening a Spanish bank account

A Spanish account is essential for empadronamiento, school fees, utilities, and many landlords.

**Best options for UK arrivals:**

| Bank | Notes |
|---|---|
| **BBVA** | "Cuenta Online sin Comisiones"; easy to open with NIE; English app |
| **Santander Spain** | Familiar to many UK expats (own UK arm); branches everywhere |
| **CaixaBank** | Originally Catalan but now national; "Hola Bank" is their expat-focused brand |
| **Bankinter** | Smaller bank with strong digital offering; good for expats and SMEs |
| **Sabadell** | Catalan bank; UK arm (TSB) makes onboarding easier; English-language support |
| **N26 / Revolut / Wise** | Neobanks; useful in interim; accepted by most landlords/utilities |
| **Openbank (Santander digital)** | Fully online; competitive; English available |

**Non-resident vs resident accounts:**
- **Non-resident** account can be opened with just a passport and NIE — useful before you have empadronamiento
- **Resident** account is opened once you have TIE; lower fees and full services
- Transition typically takes 1 visit to the branch

### Documents typically needed
- Passport
- NIE (or TIE)
- Proof of address (utility bill, lease, empadronamiento certificate)
- Proof of income / employment / tax residency declaration

## 8. Spanish language

### Do you need Spanish?

**Spanish is much more important than French was in Paris** — but Madrid is also more English-tolerant than other Spanish cities for international professional roles.

- **International firms (PE, big consultancies, IQVIA, McKinsey)**: English-only workflow is normal
- **Spanish employers (AECID, ISCIII, Spanish PE)**: B2+ Spanish strongly preferred
- **Daily life**: B1 Spanish makes everything dramatically easier; A2 is the practical minimum
- **Healthcare and bureaucracy**: Many GPs and admin staff in central Madrid speak basic English; the local police station for TIE biometrics often does not

### Levels by context

| Context | Minimum level | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| International PE / consulting / tech | English only often fine | Spanish helps for client work |
| UN, OECD-adjacent, ISCIII research | B1–B2 Spanish | English working language for many roles |
| Spanish public/private hospital data team | B2 Spanish | Required |
| Self-employed serving Spanish clients | B2 Spanish | Required for invoicing/contracts |
| Daily life | A2–B1 | Functional Spanish |
| Parent at international school | English only | Most are fully English-language |
| Parent at bilingual public school | B1–B2 Spanish | Spanish required for parent-teacher comms |

### Certifications
- **DELE (Diploma de Español como Lengua Extranjera)** — official Instituto Cervantes diploma (A1–C2)
- **SIELE** — newer, faster online certification (joint Cervantes/UNAM/Salamanca)

### Learning resources in Madrid
- **Instituto Cervantes / Escuelas Oficiales de Idiomas (EOI)** — public, very cheap, high-quality
- **Don Quijote / Enforex** — private schools, intensive courses
- **AIL Madrid** — strong private school for adult learners
- **Babbel / Duolingo / Pimsleur** — pre-arrival foundation
- **Italki / Preply** — 1-1 online tutors

## 9. Taxes & Employment

### Income tax (without Beckham Law)
| Income (€/year) | Marginal rate |
|---|---|
| 0–12,450 | 19% |
| 12,450–20,200 | 24% |
| 20,200–35,200 | 30% |
| 35,200–60,000 | 37% |
| 60,000–300,000 | 45% |
| 300,000+ | 47% |

Madrid Community adds a regional component but is one of the **lowest-tax regions in Spain** (Madrid's regional band is more favourable than Catalonia, for example).

### With Beckham Law (HQP, DNV)
- **24% flat rate** on Spanish-source employment income up to €600,000
- **47% on income above €600,000**
- 6 years of eligibility
- Spouse + children under 25 included (Startup Law 2026 update)

### Social security
- Employee: ~6.4% of gross salary
- Employer: ~30% of gross salary
- **Autónomo (self-employed)**: €80/month tarifa plana for year 1 → ~€300–€500/month from year 2 depending on income bracket (since 2023 reform, tied to actual income)

### UK–Spain double taxation treaty
- Prevents you being taxed twice on the same income
- You file in Spain on Spanish-source income; UK pension/property income may still be reportable

### VAT (IVA)
- Standard rate: 21%
- Reduced (food, transport): 10%
- Super-reduced (basic food, books, medicine): 4%
- Quarterly returns for autónomos (Modelo 303)

## 10. Quick checklist — First 3 months in Madrid

- [ ] Get **NIE** (Número de Identidad de Extranjero) — apply from UK consulate or in Spain
- [ ] Open Spanish bank account (start with N26/Revolut, move to BBVA/Santander once you have NIE)
- [ ] Find accommodation (start with furnished rental for first 6 months)
- [ ] Register at the **Padrón Municipal** (empadronamiento) at your local Junta Municipal — unlocks everything else
- [ ] Convert visa to **TIE** at the police station within 30 days of arrival
- [ ] Register at the **Centro de Salud** for Tarjeta Sanitaria
- [ ] Register dog at Junta Municipal (within 30 days) and get Spanish EU Pet Passport
- [ ] Register child at the empadronamiento + apply for nursery / school place (now or for September)
- [ ] Set up utilities: **Iberdrola / Endesa / Naturgy** (electricity & gas), **Movistar / Vodafone / Orange / Másmóvil** (internet & mobile)
- [ ] Get Madrid Transport pass (**Abono Transporte**) at any metro station; family discount if both parents on the same pass; under-7s travel free
- [ ] Apply for **Beckham Law** (within 6 months of social security registration if eligible)
- [ ] Update HMRC: P85 form to declare leaving UK + CA3837 if remaining self-employed via DNV
- [ ] Set up Spanish mobile number (Pepephone, Lowi, Yoigo, Másmóvil are good budget MVNOs)
- [ ] Join local expat/parent groups (see community.md)

# Visa Options for UK Citizens Moving to Madrid — Two Households
**Date researched: 2026-05-23**

This is the headline section for the Madrid move. Two households are moving together, with quite different visa needs:

- **Household A — Calum & Rose** (with one dog). Calum is an evaluation expert in education and public health; Rose works in health data at Oxford.
- **Household B — Momina & Jack Watton** (with one dog and a son aged ~1). Momina is **self-employed** running her own business; Jack works in **private equity**.

The right visa for each household is different. This file covers every route relevant to either household, with a recommendation per person at the end.

---

## Overview

Since Brexit (effective 1 January 2021), UK nationals no longer have the right to live or work in Spain beyond 90 days in any 180-day period. A UK passport alone is **not enough**. To live and work in Spain long-term you must obtain a **long-stay visa** from a Spanish consulate (BLS Spain in London/Manchester/Edinburgh) before you arrive, and then convert it into a **TIE (Tarjeta de Identidad de Extranjero)** residence card once in Spain.

The good news: Spain has multiple well-established pathways that fit each member of this group. The **Digital Nomad Visa** and **Highly Qualified Professional (HQP)** routes are the two most likely fits.

> **Headline answer (subject to job offers and contracts):**
> - **Calum** → HQP visa (if employer hires under article 71) or Digital Nomad Visa
> - **Rose** → accompany Calum as family, or apply for her own HQP
> - **Jack** → HQP via PE firm in Madrid, or EU Blue Card if senior
> - **Momina** → Digital Nomad Visa (remote self-employment) **or** Autónomo (Self-Employed) Visa if business is Spain-based

## Route 1 — Highly Qualified Professional (HQP) Visa
**Best for: Calum (if hired), Jack (PE role), Rose (if hired into pharma/data)**

The HQP visa is Spain's flagship route for skilled employees with a job offer from a Spanish employer. It is the closest equivalent to France's Talent Passport.

### Eligibility
- A degree or postgraduate qualification, **or** 3+ years' relevant experience
- A binding job offer from a Spanish employer
- The role must qualify as scientific, intellectual, technical, executive or managerial

### Salary thresholds (2026)
| Category | Minimum gross salary |
|---|---|
| Scientific, intellectual or technical professional (article 71) | €40,077/year |
| Executive or managerial position | €54,142/year |
| Qualifying professionals under 30 | €30,500/year |

### Processing and validity
- Application is filed by the **employer** with the UGE-CE (Spain's central immigration unit), then the visa is collected at the consulate
- Processing: ~20 business days for UGE-CE + ~10 working days at the consulate
- **Initial validity: 3 years** (renewable)
- After 1 year you exchange the visa stamp for a **TIE** at the police station in Madrid
- Family members (spouse and children) can accompany under the family-reunification provisions built into the HQP route

### Pros / cons
- ✓ Long initial validity (3 years vs 1-year initial under standard routes)
- ✓ Family route built in
- ✓ Compatible with the **Beckham Law** (24% flat-rate tax — see Taxes section)
- ✗ Requires an employer willing to sponsor; SMEs sometimes baulk at the paperwork

## Route 2 — Digital Nomad Visa (DNV)
**Best for: Momina (self-employed), and possibly Rose if she can work remotely for a UK employer**

The DNV launched in early 2023 under the Startup Law. It is designed for non-EU citizens working **remotely** — either as employees of a non-Spanish company, or self-employed serving clients outside Spain.

### Eligibility
- Working remotely for a company (or as self-employed) located outside Spain
- If self-employed, you may also work for Spanish clients, but **Spanish-client work must not exceed 20%** of total professional activity
- Degree from a recognised institution **or** 3+ years' relevant experience
- The employer/business must have existed for at least 1 year
- Clean criminal record (UK ACRO certificate)

### Income threshold (2026)
- **Primary applicant: €2,849/month gross (€34,188/year)** — 200% of the Spanish SMI
- **First family member added: +75% of SMI** (~€1,068/month)
- **Each further family member: +25% of SMI** (~€356/month per dependent)

For Momina + Jack + their son (if applying as a family unit under one DNV — though Jack and Momina will likely each have their own visa) the indicative total minimum is around €4,300/month combined.

### UK National Insurance / Spanish social security
- UK self-employed applicants must either:
  - obtain a **CA3837 certificate** from HMRC to keep paying UK NI while temporarily self-employed abroad, **OR**
  - register with Spanish social security as **RETA** (Régimen Especial de Trabajadores Autónomos)

### Tax advantage
- DNV holders are eligible for the **Beckham Law** (24% flat tax on Spanish-source income up to €600,000) for 6 years if they meet the criteria — a major saving over standard Spanish tax bands (up to 47%)

### Processing and validity
- Application can be filed **in Spain** (Tourist visa entry → conversion) or **at the consulate** in the UK before arrival
- Processing: ~20 business days from UGE-CE
- Initial visa: 1 year → can be exchanged for a 3-year **TIE** once in Spain → renewable for 2 more years

### Pros / cons
- ✓ No Spanish employer needed
- ✓ Family included from day one with a clear income uplift formula
- ✓ Compatible with Beckham Law (24% flat tax)
- ✗ Income must be primarily from outside Spain
- ✗ Momina's business will need to evidence non-Spanish revenue mix and minimum trading history

## Route 3 — EU Blue Card
**Best for: Jack if his PE role is at senior associate / VP level or above**

Spain's EU Blue Card route is for highly skilled workers earning above a higher threshold. The benefit is **intra-EU mobility** after 18 months in Spain — useful for someone in PE who may want to rotate between London, Frankfurt and Madrid.

### Key terms
- Salary threshold: ~1.5× average Spanish gross salary (roughly **€55,000–€60,000/year**, updated annually)
- Qualification: degree of 3+ years' study OR 5+ years' senior experience
- Validity: 1–4 years, renewable
- After 18 months: can move to another EU member state on a simplified procedure

For a PE associate in their late 20s / early 30s, the HQP route is usually easier; for a VP / Principal-level PE role, the Blue Card unlocks better mobility.

## Route 4 — Self-Employed (Autónomo) Visa
**Best for: Momina if her business is, or will be, primarily Spain-based**

If Momina's business is going to register in Spain and serve Spanish customers (rather than continue remote-only), the Autónomo Visa is the alternative to the DNV.

### Eligibility
- 18+, no EU/EEA/Swiss nationality, clean criminal record
- A **business plan** approved and stamped by one of Spain's recognised autónomo associations (UPTA, ATA or UATAE)
- Proof of sufficient personal funds (at least 100% of IPREM ≈ €7,200/year for a single applicant in 2026) **plus** business capital sufficient for the first year of operations

### Process
- File at the Spanish consulate in the UK (BLS Spain)
- 3 months after arrival: register with Spanish social security (RETA), then apply for the TIE within 1 month

### Validity
- 1-year initial visa, then renewable

### Cost
- ~£540 in fees, plus translation/apostille and medical certificate
- Sworn translations: ~£90 per document
- ACRO criminal certificate: £65 + ~£45 to apostille

### Pros / cons
- ✓ Fully independent business setup in Spain
- ✓ Path to permanent residency after 5 years
- ✗ Business plan must be vetted by an autónomo association — slower than DNV
- ✗ Quarterly VAT and income tax filings; mandatory monthly social security contribution (€80/month for first year via the *tarifa plana* discount, then ~€300/month)

## Route 5 — Non-Lucrative Visa (NLV)
**Best for: nobody in this group as their primary route, but useful as a backup / fallback**

The NLV is for those who can support themselves **without working in Spain**. It's most often used by retirees, but is occasionally a useful stop-gap for accompanying partners who haven't yet secured a visa in their own right.

### Eligibility (2026)
- Annual passive income or capital: **€28,800** for primary applicant
- Each dependent (spouse, child): **+€7,200/year**
- For a couple: ~**€36,000/year**
- For a family of three (e.g. Momina + Jack + child): ~**€43,200/year**
- Comprehensive private health insurance for everyone
- Clean criminal record, medical certificate

### Validity
- 1-year initial visa → 2-year renewals
- After 5 years: permanent residency option

### Why it's not the main route here
- **You cannot work in Spain on an NLV** (it's "non-lucrative"). This rules it out for Calum, Rose, Jack, and Momina if her business needs to keep operating.
- BUT: it's the cleanest **backup** for an accompanying partner who isn't ready to apply for their own work visa.

---

## Route 6 — Job-Seeker Visa
**Backup / interim route only**

Spain launched a Job-Seeker Visa for non-EU citizens who want to come to Spain to look for work or to set up a business. Valid for up to 12 months, no job offer required at application. Could be useful for Rose or Calum if they want to relocate before securing employment.

## Step-by-step process (DNV — Momina's likely route)

1. **Confirm income** — at least 12 months' bank statements + accountant's letter showing €34,188+/year gross from outside Spain
2. **Business documents** — UK Companies House extract showing 1+ year of trading, client contracts/invoices
3. **Personal documents** — passport (6+ months validity), ACRO criminal certificate (apostilled and sworn-translated), medical certificate from UK GP, NIE application form
4. **Health insurance** — private comprehensive health insurance valid throughout Spain with no co-payments and no waiting periods (DKV, Sanitas and Adeslas all offer DNV-compliant policies from ~€60/month/adult)
5. **Apply** — either at BLS Spain (London/Manchester/Edinburgh) or by entering Spain as a tourist and applying online to UGE-CE within 90 days
6. **Receive visa** — typically 20 business days from UGE-CE plus consulate time
7. **Travel to Spain** — within 90 days of visa issuance
8. **Register at the town hall (empadronamiento)** — required for everything else
9. **Apply for NIE/TIE** — at the local police station within 30 days; biometrics taken; card arrives 3–4 weeks later
10. **Register with Spanish social security (RETA)** **or** file CA3837 with HMRC

---

## Step-by-step process (HQP — Calum/Jack)

1. **Job offer** meeting the salary threshold (€40,077+ for technical / €54,142+ for managerial)
2. **Employer files** the HQP application with UGE-CE
3. UGE-CE issues authorisation (~20 business days)
4. **You apply for the visa** at the Spanish consulate (BLS Spain) — ~10 working days
5. Visa is stamped in passport
6. **Travel to Spain** within 90 days
7. **Empadronamiento** at the town hall
8. **TIE application** at the police station (cita previa needed)
9. Family members file Family Reunification HQP simultaneously or shortly after

## Children — Momina & Jack's son

The HQP, DNV and Autónomo visas all allow children to be included from day one as accompanying family members. For a child under one:

- **Apply at the same time** as the parents (one combined visa application)
- Include the child's full UK birth certificate (apostilled and sworn-translated)
- Comprehensive private health insurance covering the child
- After arrival in Madrid, register the child on the **empadronamiento** with both parents
- Children under 7 ride Madrid public transport free; children get a heavily subsidised annual abono transport pass after that

## Practical tips

- **Start the apostille and translation process 3 months before applying** — the ACRO certificate alone can take 2–4 weeks
- **HQP is filed by the employer; DNV by you** — that's the key practical difference; if you're sponsoring yourself you'll want DNV or Autónomo
- **Beckham Law is opt-in** — you have 6 months from social security registration to apply. Worth claiming if any of you earn above ~€60k.
- **Health insurance must be DNV/NLV-compliant** — meaning no co-payments and no waiting periods. Standard expat policies often won't meet this; check the small print
- **Family route**: Spouse + children are added to the *primary* applicant's file under HQP and DNV — don't apply separately if you can avoid it
- **Cita previa**: All in-Spain immigration appointments require an online appointment (cita previa). They book up fast; use specialist services like [https://www.tieinspain.com](https://www.tieinspain.com) or schedule from the UK before travel
